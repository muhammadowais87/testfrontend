Great Caliphs of Islam
1.	The first caliphate, Rashidun Caliphate, was ruled by the four Rashidun (rightly-guided) caliphs: Hazrat Abu Bakr ?, Hazrat Umar ?, Hazrat Usman ? and Hazrat Ali ?. Caliphs were religious and political leaders to Muslim world. They were close companions of Hazrat Muhammad ?. They ruled under the guidance of the Holy Quran and teachings of Hazrat Muhammad ?. The Rashidun Caliphate lasted for 30 years. It is a role model for all Muslim rulers.
2.	Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique ? was a friend of Hazrat Muhammad ?. His name was Abdullah and his kunniyat (family name) was Abu Bakr. His title was Siddique. He was a man of good habits. He was very kind hearted and loving. He was the first among men to accept Islam. Hazrat Abu Bakr spent his whole life in the company of Rasoolullah ?. He has great love and affection for him.
3.	Hazrat Umar ? was elected as the second caliph of Islam. Before accepting Islam, Hazrat Umar ? was an opponent of Islam. Hazrat Muhammad ? wished that Umar ? must accept Islam. Allah answered his prayers and guided him ? to accepting Islam. The Muslims gained strength after Hazrat Umar ? accepted Islam. Earlier, the Muslims would say their prayers in hiding, but later they began to pray inside the Kaaba.
4.	Hazrat Usman ? was a very pious man. He was a friend of Hazrat Abu Bakr ?. When Hazrat Abu Bakr ? invited him to Islam, he accepted it instantly. Hazrat Usman ? was a very rich trader. He was known as “Ghani” because he was very generous and spent generously in the way of Allah. He always helped the Muslims in times of need. He was known for marrying two of Hazrat Muhammad’s ? daughters, both at different times.
5.	Hazrat Ali ? was the son of Hazrat Abu Talib, uncle of Hazrat Muhammad ?. From his early childhood, Hazrat Ali ? lived with Rasoolullah ?. Hence, he was brought up in the care of Rasoolullah ?. Hazrat Ali ? was the first among children to accept Islam. He was at that time only ten years old. Hazrat Ali ? wrote letters treaties for Rasoolullah ?. He was a very courageous man. That is why, he is also known as ‘Asadullah’, meaning ‘the Lion of Allah’.
The Journey of Chocolate
6.	We have all eaten chocolate but not many of us know where it comes from or how it is made. Historians say chocolate has been around for almost 2000 years. In ancient times, chocolate was a symbol of wealth as only the rich could afford it. Chocolate came from Central and South America to Spain in the 1500s through Spanish explorers who brought it back with them. It became very popular and quickly spread throughout Europe.
7.	It takes the pod about six months to ripen. After that, the pods are collected into heaps and then split open to remove the beans. Now the process of delicious chocolate making starts. First of all. The beans are heated or placed in the hot sun for drying. Then they are sent to factories. Chocolate beans are cleaned before they are roasted in a big oven. Next the shells are removed from the beans. The remaining pieces are edible and called ‘nibs’.
8.	The nibs are roasted and mixed with sugar and made into a paste. Dried milk and some flavours are added to the mixture. This mixture is then poured into big trays. A granite roller is pushed in each tray for several days. The chocolate now becomes a fine liquid. Finally, this liquid is left to harden. This is how chocolate is made. Colours of Pakistan
15.	Pakistan is a country with a rich cultural heritage. It is comprised of different cultural groups like Punjabis, Sindhis, Balochis, Pakhtuns, Kashmiris and Gilgit-Baltistanis. These cultural groups reflect a range of amazing traditions, dresses, foods and languages. People from different cultural groups speak a variety of languages. In Punjab, people speak ‘Punjabi’ and ‘Seraiki’. ‘Sindhi’ and ‘Balochi’ are common languages of Sindh and Balochistan. In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, people speak ‘Pashto’, ‘Hindko’, ‘Khowar’ and ‘Seraiki’.
16.	The culture of the Punjab is considered one of the oldest and richest cultures in the world. Punjab has always been the land of peace, love and great cultural and artistic values. Punjabi men wear kurta, tahmat and turban. Shalwar kameez or kurta are Punjabi women’s traditional dress. A seasonal festival named Jashan-e-Baharan or the Spring festival is celebrated in Punjab in the mid of February. The culture of Sindh evolved from the Indus Valley Civilisation.
17.	The culture of Balochistan is full of traditions, arts and crafts. Balochistan is also known for its tribes and festivals. Poetry and storytelling are very popular among the Baloch: Balochis wear various styles of kameez, shalwar, turban (kullah) and head scarfs. “Phashik” is a traditional Balochi dress. In Balochistan, the Sibi festival attracts people from all across the country. Hospitality, hard work and bravery are distinctive features of Pakhrun culture. Pakhtuns follow Pakhtunwali, that is, a traditional life style.
18.	Shinas and Balti cultures exist in Gilgit-Baltistan. The people of Baltistan are very cheerful and hospitable. Men usually wear a woolen cap, shalwar kameez and woolen robe with long and short sleeves. Women wear traditional Iraghi caps and colourful frocks. The Harvest festival is celebrated every year in Gilgit-Baltistan. Islamic values and teachings are common factors shared by all cultures of Pakistan. A number of religious and regional festivals are celebrated here.
