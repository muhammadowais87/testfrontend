The Apple Tree and the Farmer
1.	Once there was a farmer, who lived in a village. He had a big garden. There was an old apple tree and some other trees in the garden. In his childhood, he used to spend most of his time playing under the apple tree. It would give him healthy apples to eat. As time passed, the apple tree grew old. Now, it couldn't give apples to the farmer anymore.
2.	In the evening, the farmer gave a final thought to his decision. As he took his axe and began chopping the tree, all the animals and birds came rushing down. They all started pleading with the farmer. They gathered around him and requested, "Please don't cut the tree! This is our home." The farmer was stubborn. He raised his axe and the fuss grew. "Please, don't cut it! This is my home," cried the squirrel. "Please, don't chop and destroy our nests!" cried the little birds. "Please, don't cut the apple tree," pleaded the grasshopper.
3.	Realising the change in the farmer's attitude, the little animals spoke in unity. The bee promised him to always provide honey. The birds offered to sing songs for him. Finally, the farmer realised his mistake. Allah had made him realise that the old apple tree was home to many lovely animals who were his childhood friends. He asked Allah for forgiveness. The farmer threw away the axe and promised all the animals never to cut that apple tree down. The animals thanked Allah. They continued living happily together.
A Visit to the Seaside
4.	One of my mother's cousins, Aunt Iqra, had invited us to her daughter's wedding in Karachi. I was very happy when my father confirmed the plan to attend the wedding. We planned to stay there for a week. We set off for Karachi by train. We reached there after a long, tiring journey. However, it was fun watching the beautiful landscape as the train passed through the green fields.
5.	When the wedding was over, I told my parents about my wish to visit the seaside. My dad happily agreed to take us to Hawke's Bay. A strong sea breeze was blowing there. The waves crashing on the shore. It was a lovely sight to watch. I walked with my cousins and I began to walk on the soft sand. I collected some beautiful seashells and stones. We were enjoying it when I saw something blue there.
Hazrat Abu Bakr
6.	Hazrat Muhammad (SAW) changed his name to Abdullah (the slave of Allah). Hazrat Abu Bakr accompanied Hazrat Muhammad (SAW) at the time of migration to Madinah. There, they spent three nights in the Cave of Thawr. A snake stung Hazrat Abu Bakr, while Rasooluullah was asleep with his head in his lap.
7.	He chose not to disturb Hazrat Muhammad (SAW) and let the snake sting him repeatedly. He was a trader by profession. He was well-known for his honesty and fair dealings in business. He used to help people generously by all his means. His good habits and fine deeds made him famous and people started calling him Abu Bakr (the man who comes forward in good deeds).
8.	His daughter, Hazrat Ayesha, was married to Rasoolullah (SAW). He led his whole life according to the teachings of Islam. He always helped Rasoolullah (SAW) and devoted himself to the cause of Islam. He sacrificed all his wealth in the battle of Tabuk. He was one of those companions of Rasoolullah (SAW) who used to write down the verses of the Holy Quran whenever a verse was revealed.
Unity is Strength
9.	Once there was a flock of doves led by their king. One day, it flew a long distance in search of food. All the doves got tired but found no food. The king prayed to Allah and encouraged his companions to try a little more. Soon they found some rice grains scattered under a banyan tree. All the doves were happy to find the food and thanked Allah.
10.	However, the king didn't lose hope and had an idea. He knew that their unity and Allah's help would give them the strength to get out of this situation. He advised all the doves to fly together carrying the net with them. All the doves obeyed him and tried to fly. Each dove picked up a portion of the net and they all flew up together, carrying the net with them. The hunter ran after them but he couldn't catch the flock of doves.
11.	Our next task is to get out of this net. A mouse who lives on the nearby hill is a good friend of mine. Let's go to him and ask for help," said the king. They all flew to that hill and the king told the whole matter to the mouse. He requested the mouse to nibble the net so they could set themselves free. The mouse agreed to help them and started nibbling the net around the king.

The Wise Man Who Never Lied
15.	Once there lived a very wise man who never ever lied. His name was Hammad. He was famous for always telling the truth. The headman of the town heard about Hammad and ordered his men to call for him. When they returned with Hammad, the headman asked, "Hammad, is it true that you have never lied?" "Yes, it's true and I will never lie in my life," replied Hammad.
16.	Several days passed and the headman called Hammad once again to assess him by putting him in a tricky situation. Having a big crowd all around, the headman talked to Hammad pretending as if he was going hunting. With one foot in his horse's stirrup, he ordered Hammad, "Go to my place and tell my son I will be with him to arrange a big feast. You will have lunch with me then."
17.	The next day, people gathered at the headman's place. They were anxiously waiting for the headman to come. Soon he came and accused Hammad arrogantly in the presence of the people, "The wise man, who never lies, lied to my son yesterday." However, his son told him about the words of Hammad. The headman realised that the wise man never lied and said only what he saw with his own eyes.
Manchester of Pakistan
18.	Faisalabad is the third most populous and one of the major industrial cities in Pakistan. Faisalabad was formerly called Lyallpur. In 1977, the city was named Faisalabad after the name of Shah Faisal bin Abdul Aziz, the late king of Saudi Arabia. It is also known as Manchester of Pakistan because it is the centre of trade and the biggest textile city of Pakistan as same in Manchester in UK.
19.	It is also famous for its food. Desi food and snacks like samosas, dahhi bhally and gol gappy are its specialties. Biryani and pulao are also very well-known. There are lots of attractive places found all around Faisalabad which are a source of attraction for visitors and native people. The Faisalabad clock tower is famous over the world and is one of the oldest monuments. It is situated in the old part of the city and is very popular all over the country.
20.	It is about 300 ft tall The tower has eight bazaars around it: Katchery Bazaar, Chiniot Bazaar, Jhang Bazaar, Bhawana Bazaar, Aminpur Bazaar and Rail Bazaar. People from different cities of Pakistan come here for shopping. The city exports fabric to many countries. Faisalabad is also a hub of literature and Education. Many renowned poets and prose writers belong to it.
Let's Plan a School Trip
21.	One day, Samia and Fahad were talking to each other. Fahad told his sister about his friend's school trip. Fahad's friend told him that he had an amazing experience and learnt a lot of new things during his school trip at the chocolate factory. Samia listened to her brother carefully and asked him to plan a school trip to a factory.
22.	The next day, they went to school and discussed it with their class teacher Miss Hina. She liked their idea and asked the class to give suggestions about places to visit for their school trip. They decided to visit a shoe factory near their school, but first they had to take permission from the manager of the factory. So, Fahad wrote a letter to the manager of the factory.
23.	After a few days, Fahad got a reply from Mr Adnan. He granted permission for their visit. The class was happy upon hearing the news. The next week, they visited the shoe factory with their class teacher. They were amazed to see how shoes were made. They collected information and shared it with their families.
Evening
24.	The day is past, the sun is set, And the white stars are in the sky; While the long grass with dew is wet, and through the air the bats now fly. The lambs have now lain down to sleep, the birds have long since sought their nests, the air is still and dark and deep on the hillside the old wood rests. Yet of the dark I have no fear, but feel as safe as when it's light; for I know God is with me there, and He will guard me through the night.
